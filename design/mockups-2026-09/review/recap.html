The Stickmen Hub · plan recap

What's coming, in order

Everything below is agreed and written down. It covers two goals: make the hub look and feel professionally made, then put it on the App Store for iPad first and iPhone second.

Where we are: the plan and the design are finished and approved. Nothing has been built yet. One check remains before building starts: an engineering review of the plan.

The six steps

  1. Smooth and sharp games

    Four of the seven games look blurry on an iPad screen, and two use a slow glow effect. All seven get one shared engine piece that makes them crisp, steady at 60 frames a second, and gives sound on the first tap. This comes first because you said no lag matters most.

  2. Each game gets its own colours

    The store, leaderboards and buttons keep the same layout everywhere, but take the colours of the game you opened them from. A new game gets this for free by adding three colours to one file.

  3. One front door for every game

    Today each game built its own start screen. They all move to one shared start screen: cover, title, mode buttons, Play. Each still looks like its own game.

  4. A new home screen

    A big featured game on top, all games in a row you swipe, and your progress at the bottom. Light mode is the storybook look. Dark mode is the same layout in neon arcade colours.

  5. Play first, sign in later

    Anyone can open the app and play. Sign-in buttons appear in six places, and after every game it asks "Save this score? Create an account". Scores earned as a guest are kept when they sign up.

  6. The App Store

    The same app is wrapped for iPhone and iPad and built in the cloud, so no Mac is needed. Friends test it through TestFlight first, then it goes to the store.

Small things you chose

  • The top bar has five things: logo, coins, Store, Leaderboards, and your avatar. Everything else is in the avatar menu.
  • A round Stickmen Hub sticker sits on the featured picture, and the name in the bar is bigger.
  • The featured game changes every visit and says why, like "You haven't tried this one".
  • Tapping a mode button such as Hard Mode starts the game in that mode.
  • Cards squish and glow when you press them, on iPad, phone and computer.
  • Dark panels are solid, not frosted glass, so older iPads stay smooth.
  • Every screen has a planned look for loading, empty, offline and "can't afford it".
  • The phone gets its own layout with a Play button that stays under your thumb.
  • Every button gets a spoken label for the screen reader, and text grows with the iPad's text size.

The App Store part

Built for you

  • A new sign-in method that works inside an app
  • A Delete account button (Apple requires it)
  • Icons bundled in the app so it works offline
  • Taps that buzz, and layouts that respect the notch
  • The cloud build that makes the app without a Mac

Only you can do

  • Create the app record in App Store Connect
  • Make the Apple key and add it to GitHub as a secret
  • Answer the age rating and privacy questions
  • Upload the icon, screenshots and description
  • Give Apple a test login and press Submit

Still open

  • Which email service sends password resets, and whether that is in the first version.
  • Where the privacy policy page lives.
  • Painted covers for Archery, Riposte, Genesis and Orbit Dodge, which are still simple drawings.
  • A public store listing, or an unlisted link for friends only.

What happens next

Say "run the engineering review" and the plan gets its last check. After that, building starts with step 1, beginning with Echo, and you get a before and after video on the iPad.
