Hub redesign — three directions

Each mockup is one iPad-landscape screen (1366×1024, also fine at 1180×820): top bar, featured hero, horizontal shelf of all seven games, journey/streak row. Below the fold, each page shows the shared Store panel twice — hub skin and Echo skin — to prove the "same chrome, per-game theme" idea in that direction.

A — Painted Storybook

Cream paper panels · 2px ink outlines · stickers · Fredoka at hand-lettered sizes

Doubles down on what already makes the hub charming: the illustrated blue-sky plate and hand-drawn covers. Panels become cream pages with a paper grain, thick ink outlines and a hard drop, chips tilt a few degrees, and every shelf tile wears a sticker in its game's accent. It is the most "family" of the three and the closest evolution of the current look; the Echo store keeps the cream page but takes a cave-navy header band, cyan glows on item tiles and cyan buttons, so per-game theming reads as a re-inked page rather than a new screen. Risk: the strongest personality, so it needs the most discipline to still read as "professional" rather than "craft fair".

Open Direction A

B — Neon Arcade

Dark glass · luminous 1px edges · accent glows · uppercase tracked Fredoka

Treats the hub like a cabinet in a dark arcade: the sky plate is dimmed to 18% behind blurred glass panels, and all light comes from the games themselves — purple on the bar, lavender on the full-bleed hero (with a static progress ring and particle dots), and a glowing underline in each game's accent under its shelf tile. This is the direction that most obviously says "game launcher", and it makes the existing dark theme the primary theme instead of an afterthought. The Echo store is the same dark panel with its light source swapped to cave-cyan: cyan border glow, cyan-filled buttons with dark text. Risk: it is the least legible in bright rooms on an iPad, and covers with white backgrounds (Archery, Riposte) need a dark matte to sit well.

Open Direction B

C — Clean Console

Near-white surface · 28px tiles · hairlines · one solid accent block per game

Borrows the calm of Apple Arcade and the eShop: a near-white surface with only an 8% wash of the sky plate at the top, big rounded white tiles with generous whitespace, thin hairlines and one restrained shadow. Titles are big bold Fredoka in ink with no tricks; the only strong colour on the shelf is a solid block in each game's accent behind its cover, so seven very different covers line up as one collection. The Echo store is the same white panel whose header band turns cave-navy with cyan text and whose buttons turn cyan-on-navy, with a 6% cyan tint on item rows. This is the safest route to "professionally made" and the easiest to keep consistent across seven games; risk is that it loses some of the hand-drawn warmth that makes the current hub feel homemade in a good way.
